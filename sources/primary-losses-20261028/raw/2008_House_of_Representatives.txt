The '''2008 United States House of Representatives elections''' were held on November 4, 2008, to elect members to the [[United States House of Representatives]] to serve in the [[111th United States Congress]] from January 3, 2009, until January 3, 2011. It coincided with [[2008 United States presidential election|the election of Barack Obama as president]]. All 435 voting seats, as well as all 6 non-voting seats, were up for election. The [[Democratic Party (United States)|Democratic Party]], which won a majority of seats in the 2006 election, expanded its control in 2008.

The [[Republican Party (United States)|Republican Party]], hoping to regain the majority it lost in the 2006 election or at least expand its congressional membership, lost additional seats. With one exception ([[Louisiana's 2nd congressional district|Louisiana's 2nd district]]), the only seats to switch from Democratic to Republican had been Republican-held prior to the 2006 elections. Republicans gained five Democratic seats total, while losing 26 of their own, giving the Democrats a net gain of 21 seats, effectively erasing all gains made by the GOP since [[1994 United States House of Representatives elections|1994]].<ref>{{cite news|last1=Hagerty|first1=Barbara|last2=Tedford|first2=Deborah|title=Democrats Poised For Landslide Wins In House|newspaper=[[NPR]]|date=November 4, 2008|location=|url=https://www.npr.org/2008/11/04/96511716/democrats-poised-for-landslide-wins-in-house|access-date=June 28, 2026}}</ref> In addition, with the defeat of Republican congressman [[Chris Shays]] in [[Connecticut's 4th congressional district|Connecticut's 4th district]], this became the first time since the 1850s that no Republican represented the [[New England|New England region]].<ref>{{cite news|last=Haigh|first=Susan|title=GOP a dying breed in New England|newspaper=[[USA Today]]|date=November 9, 2008|location=|url=http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/news/politics/2008-11-09-4237129098_x.htm|access-date=June 28, 2026}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|last=|first=|title=Jim Himes Defeats Christopher Hayes In 4th District|newspaper=[[Hartford Courant]]|date=November 5, 2008|location=|url=https://www.courant.com/2008/11/05/jim-himes-defeats-christopher-shays-in-4th-district/|url-access=subscription|access-date=June 28, 2026}}</ref>

The 10.6% popular vote advantage by the Democrats was the largest by either party since [[1982 United States House of Representatives elections|1982]], 26 years earlier, and as of 2026 remains the most recent time that either party won by a double-digit margin in the overall popular vote for the House of Representatives.<ref>{{cite news|last=Enten|first=Harry|title=The most underdiscussed fact of the 2022 election: how historically close it was|newspaper=[[CNN]]|date=December 26, 2022|location=|url=https://www.cnn.com/2022/12/26/politics/midterm-election-2022-historically-close/index.html|access-date=June 28, 2026}}</ref> Turnout increased due to the [[2008 United States presidential election|concurrent presidential election]].

{{As of|2026}}, this remains the last election in which Democrats won House seats in [[2008 United States House of Representatives elections in Idaho|Idaho]], [[2008 United States House of Representatives election in North Dakota|North Dakota]], and [[2008 United States House of Representatives election in South Dakota|South Dakota]]; and the last election in which Democrats won a majority of seats in [[2008 United States House of Representatives elections in Arkansas|Arkansas]], [[2008 United States House of Representatives elections in Indiana|Indiana]], [[2008 United States House of Representatives elections in Mississippi|Mississippi]], [[2008 United States House of Representatives elections in Ohio|Ohio]], [[2008 United States House of Representatives elections in Tennessee|Tennessee]], [[2008 United States House of Representatives elections in West Virginia|West Virginia]], and [[2008 United States House of Representatives elections in Wisconsin|Wisconsin]]. It is also the last time Republicans won the [[2008 United States House of Representatives election in Delaware|Delaware]] seat.
